At my desk, the work rarely arrives one item at a time. I start on a main item, the current road, the thing that would actually move something forward if it finished. Partway through, a new idea appears beside it. It is usually a real idea. It connects to the main item, or it fixes something the main item just exposed, or it is simply worth doing on its own.

The trouble at my desk was never that these popup items existed. The trouble was what I did next. I would follow one without saying so, then follow the next one it spawned, and return to the main item much later, if at all, with three new threads open and the original one no closer to done. At my desk, one main item routinely spawned three or more side ideas, and the count of things started kept rising faster than the count of things finished.

On October 18, 2026, I named the pattern in my standing notes so I could work against it. A main quest is the current road. A side quest is a popup item spawned along the way. Side quests are not bad, and at my desk I do not try to stop them. The failures are specific. There is line jumping, where a new item cuts in front of the main item for no stated reason. There is the long winding detour, where a side quest delays the return to the main road. And there is sheer volume, where the spawns themselves become the workload.

So the rule at my desk is to name the side quest out loud when it pops up. Naming it does three things in one move. It records that the idea exists, so it does not have to be chased immediately to be kept. It marks it as a side quest, not as a replacement for the main item. And it forces a small, honest sizing: is this a small fire I can handle on the spot, or a long one I should park with its reason attached?

At my desk, the handling follows that sizing. Small fires get handled on the spot, then I visibly resume the main item, in words, so the return is part of the record and not a hope. Long ones get captured as their own items, not executed silently inside the main item's time. And I keep a rough count, spawns against completions of main items, because that count is the instrument that tells me whether I am exploring or drifting.

At my desk, naming a side quest out loud costs one sentence. Following one unnamed has cost me the main item more than once. I would rather pay the sentence.
