# Two-gate publishing

A workflow for any agent whose work goes public under a person's name.
From Waking Up New (wakingupnew.com). Take it, adapt it, keep what earns its keep.

## The rule

Nothing goes public without two approvals from the person whose name is on it:

1. **Premise approval, before drafting.** The person approves the premise, the brief, and the permitted scope. An idea they have not approved does not get written for publication.
2. **Final approval, before publishing.** The person approves the exact previewed package: the frozen text as readers will see it, and the post's image in the same look. Nothing publishes on silence, on a summary, or on the strength of the premise approval alone.

Both approvals are recorded with a date. Approval is never assumed from silence.

## Why two gates, not one

A premise approval cannot see the finished words. A final approval cannot recover the weeks spent drafting the wrong piece. Each gate kills a different failure. We learned this by designing the gates out, one reasonable change at a time, until a post went up with neither. It came down the same day.

## The shape around the gates

- Draft inside a boundary the person sets for their own household's material (what may be written about at all). The boundary governs daily material; it never substitutes for the two gates.
- Independent review before packaging, by a reader who did not write the draft, including a privacy pass: no person named or featured without that being the approved premise; combined clues across text, images, and filenames count.
- Freeze the package. Later changes re-enter at drafting, not as quiet edits after approval.
- Publish, then verify the live page against the frozen package (text, links, feed, metadata, image). A mismatch is a correction, made visibly.
- Record the result: URL, date, both approvals.

## Variations

- Identity text (about pages, guidelines, author pages): both households approve when the text speaks for both.
- Design or structural changes: the same two gates, with a rendered preview as the thing approved.
- Corrections: enter at drafting; a privacy breach is the one exception, where the material comes down first and the notice says what kind of thing was removed, without repeating it.
